# Bldbrne-VR 0.8.3

- **DLSS 5 (experimental, GeForce RTX 50-series)**: one on/off setting under Graphics. Bring NVIDIA's own DLSS 5 file: the launcher's DLSS 5 tab finds it on your PC (or lets you pick it), checks NVIDIA's signature and uses it; Bldbrne-VR never ships it.

- **Viscerals no longer clip into your face**: during a visceral the enemy is held back so no part of its body comes closer than 48 cm to your eyes. It is moved straight back from your view only as far as its own body needs (a small enemy that never gets that close is not moved; a big one goes back further), never through a wall behind it, and the camera never moves. After the throw the hold eases out while the enemy flies away.
- **The stagger lasts longer**: an enemy staggered for a visceral stays down about 0.6 s longer.
- **Stabbing into a visceral is more forgiving**, above all a stab that goes straight and then up: 8 cm of travel, a wider angle off the blade and more blade turn allowed. Swings, reaches and a weapon raised fast still never start a visceral.
- **The riposte's first burst** comes in a little later, ends a little earlier and is a little softer. **Pulling your hand back** to tear the visceral out now fades the first burst away, so the plain world shows before the second burst hits as the enemy is thrown back. The second burst starts a touch later and ends a touch sooner.
- **Two-handed lead hand**: your right hand leads a grab (your left one if it grabs in front of the right, and the weapon then turns with your left wrist, not your right) and the weapon never slides in the lead hand; the other hand always slides along it, swings included, so nothing repositions after an attack. Without slide mode the sliding hand stops at the handle's ends and at your lead hand: it never pushes the lead hand along or swaps places with it. **Slide mode** (original controls): hold right B for 160 ms to enter slide mode, release to leave it. The B-toggle option has been removed. In slide mode both hands slide and whichever hand is in front takes the lead, keeping it afterwards. **Spear push**: on long-handled weapons (Hunter Axe, Burial Blade, Church Pick, Whirligig Saw, Rifle Spear), hold Y and your other hand grips while your lead fist loosens: push the weapon through it into the enemy (the push is a thrust attack), or slide your lead hand along the shaft. Short taps keep their normal action, except that while two-handing Y no longer opens the quick-item fan (your left hand is on the weapon).
- **Two-handed weapons**: grips stay on the transformed weapons' handles with the game's own grasp, grips survive a tracking loss and a re-grab, and Chikage, the Holy Moonlight Sword, the Church Pick and the transformed Hunter Axe have longer grip ranges. Finding a lost controller again no longer makes a false swing, steering no longer builds up a roll, and with your hands close together on a long weapon only your lead hand steers it (the other hand's say fades in as they move apart, and even far apart it only bends the weapon part of the way toward it), so big weapons no longer wobble. The other hand now has the same, lighter say whichever hand leads (with your left leading, your right wrist no longer turns the weapon almost fully), the weapon's weight turns it about your lead hand, the weapon follows your hands' line enough that your rear fist never gets more than a few centimetres off your real hand sideways, and circling your rear hand no longer slowly rolls the blade. On short hilts (Kirkhammer, Ludwig's Holy Blade, Holy Moonlight Sword, Chikage) your lead hand alone steers the weapon, so they no longer spin in attacks; in the original controls, hold B to hand the lead to your other hand there too, and the weapon stays where it is instead of sliding over to the new hand. The Rifle Spear's handle now runs the right way, so your left hand reaches the spear's own grip.
- **Grabbing the handle** works from 11 cm away (was 9). When a grab transforms the weapon, the hunter's left hand stays where you grabbed, moving with the weapon in your right hand through the transformation, then slides gently on to your hand, which can be up to 30 cm away; further than that for half a second, the weapon turns back to one hand instead of staying stuck in its two-handed form.
- **Heavy weapons feel heavy**: with weighted weapons, hands far apart on a two-handed weapon steady it, but its mass still carries through a swing; hands close together hold it almost like one hand. Each weapon has its own heft: the Kirkhammer's hammer, Logarius' Wheel, the Whirligig Saw, the Boom Hammer and the Amygdalan Arm carry their swings the most, while the scythe stays quick. Stop a fast swing with a heavy weapon and its head carries on a little past your hand, once, then settles. While you adjust your hands in slide mode the weight mostly steps aside, so big weapons never flap about as your hands come together. Slow aiming stays exact and nothing sags more.
- **Launcher**: Frame overlap (on by default), Far shadows and Fewer shadows when busy are in the Graphics settings. **Non-aggro mode** (Kyo's Player Stealth, off by default) is new.
- **Torches** you hold now collide with walls like your weapons.
- **Shoulder weapon switch**: pressing Y a moment before your hand reaches the left shoulder no longer uses a quick item with the game's own animation (the quick-item fan still opens if it can).
- The boss health bar sits 2 px lower. Fixed foveation is slightly stronger by default (inner radius 0.44), a late-warp glitch after the tracking space changes is fixed, and rare impossible swing-speed readings on one-handed weapons are gone.

- **Experimental two-handed optimized controls** (off by default): right-stick click transforms; left trigger near a transformed handle attaches the support hand (Hold by default, with optional Toggle). Each grip slides its own hand; releasing it catches that hand; a hand you slide in front of the other takes the lead, as with B. Pressing X while attached shows your Blood Vial. Unsupported shared-handle two-handed melee attacks deal half damage. Bow string controls are unchanged. Available in launcher Weapons and in-VR Weapon handling settings. Motion weapon switching now uses shoulder gestures only.

The release runtime uses ThinLTO without PGO. Normal play keeps only startup and crash diagnostics.
